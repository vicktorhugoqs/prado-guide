Prado Museum FAQ

Practical answers for a first visit to the Museo del Prado. For the paintings themselves, the main guide has 54 masterpieces in walking order with room numbers, and the 2-hour route a shorter plan.

Is the Prado worth visiting?

Yes. It is one of the three or four greatest collections of European painting in the world, and for some painters — Velázquez, Goya, Bosch, Titian, Rubens, El Greco — it is the greatest anywhere, because it grew out of the Spanish royal collection, which bought them in depth. If you have time for one museum in Madrid and care about old-master painting, this is it.

How long do you need at the Prado?

Two to three hours for the highlights; a full day is not too much. Our 2-hour route covers about twenty-five masterpieces in walking order and includes a one-hour express list. Plan a break: the museum is large, and attention runs out before the pictures do.

What are the must-see paintings at the Prado?

Velázquez's Las Meninas and The Spinners; Goya's 3rd of May 1808, Family of Carlos IV, the two Majas and the Black Paintings; Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights; Van der Weyden's Descent from the Cross; Titian's Charles V at Mühlberg; El Greco's Nobleman with his Hand on his Chest; Rubens's Three Graces; Dürer's Self-Portrait; Fra Angelico's Annunciation. The main guide has 54 with room numbers.

How much are Prado tickets, and when is it free?

General admission is €15; the reduced ticket is €7.50. Entry is free for everyone in the last two hours (Monday to Saturday 18:00–20:00, Sundays and holidays 17:00–19:00), and always free for under-18s. Free hours mean queues, and the same timed tickets apply, so expect to wait at the door. Prices change — checked 24 November 2026, so confirm on the official prices page, and buy only from museodelprado.es: resellers charge more.

What are the Prado's opening hours?

Monday to Saturday 10:00–20:00; Sundays and public holidays 10:00–19:00. It closes early (14:00) on 6 January and 24 and 31 December, and is closed on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December. Always confirm on museodelprado.es, which also lists any temporary changes.

Do I need to book Prado tickets in advance?

It is wise. Tickets are sold for timed entry slots on museodelprado.es, and at busy times — weekends, holidays, the free evening hours — the queue at the ticket office can be long. Buy from the museum's own site: resellers charge more for the same ticket.

When is the Prado free?

Every day, in the last two hours before closing: Monday to Saturday 18:00–20:00, Sundays and holidays 17:00–19:00. Expect a queue; arriving at the start of the free period with a plan (see the one-hour express list) is the way to make it worth it. Under-18s are always free.

Where is Las Meninas in the Prado?

In Room 12 on Floor 1, the main floor, at the centre of the building, surrounded by Velázquez's other royal portraits. The Spinners is nearby in Room 15A. Room numbers were checked in November 2026; the paper plan at the entrance has the current hang.

Where is The Garden of Earthly Delights?

In Room 56A on Floor 0, the Bosch room, with The Haywain and the Table of the Seven Deadly Sins. Bruegel's Triumph of Death and Patinir are next door in Room 55A, Van der Weyden's Descent from the Cross in Room 58.

Where are Goya's Black Paintings and the 3rd of May?

On Floor 0. The 2nd and 3rd of May 1808 hang together in Room 64; all fourteen Black Paintings, the murals from Goya's house, are in Room 67, with his late Milkmaid of Bordeaux close by. His court portraits — the Family of Carlos IV (Room 32) and the Majas (Room 38) — are on Floor 1, and the tapestry cartoons on Floor 2.

Can you take photos inside the Prado?

No — photography and filming are not allowed in the collection galleries, and guards enforce it. That is part of why the guide gives every work an image: use it as your notebook.

Is Guernica in the Prado?

No. Picasso's Guernica is at the Museo Reina Sofía, a ten-minute walk south, beside Atocha station. It belonged to the Prado on paper for years and hung in the Casón del Buen Retiro, a Prado building, from 1981 until it moved to the Reina Sofía in 1992.

Prado, Reina Sofía or Thyssen — which should I choose?

The Prado for European painting from the 12th to the 19th century, above all Spanish, Flemish and Italian. The Reina Sofía for the 20th century — Picasso's Guernica, Dalí, Miró. The Thyssen-Bornemisza for a compact survey of everything in between, including Impressionism and Expressionism that neither of the others has. All three are on the same 'Paseo del Arte' within a fifteen-minute walk, and a combined Paseo del Arte ticket covers one visit to each.

How do I get to the Prado?

It is on the Paseo del Prado. The nearest metro stations are Banco de España (line 2) and Estación del Arte (line 1); Atocha railway station is about ten minutes' walk south. The museum has several doors — Goya, Jerónimos, Velázquez, Murillo — and your ticket tells you which to use.

What is the best time to visit the Prado?

A weekday at opening time, or mid-afternoon on a weekday, when the morning groups have gone. The free hours at the end of the day are the most crowded.

Can I see the Prado in one hour?

Yes, if you choose. Stay mostly on Floor 1: El Greco's Nobleman, Titian's Charles V, Las Meninas, The Spinners, Rubens's Three Graces, Goya's royal family and the Majas — then go down to Floor 0 for the 3rd of May and the Black Paintings. That list, with room numbers, is on our 2-hour route page.

Is there a cloakroom at the Prado?

Yes, and it is free. Large bags and backpacks have to be left there before you enter the galleries, so allow a few minutes at busy times.

Who founded the Prado?

It opened on 19 November 1819 as the Royal Museum of Paintings, under Ferdinand VII, in a building Juan de Villanueva had designed in the 1780s as a natural history museum for Carlos III. Its core is the royal collection gathered by the Habsburg and Bourbon kings since the 16th century, which is why its strengths are the painters those kings loved.
